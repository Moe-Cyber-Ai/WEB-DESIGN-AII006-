Threats & Risks

Understanding Cyber Threats and Vulnerabilities

Common Types of Cyber Threats

Cyber threats come in many forms, each with different methods and objectives. Understanding these threats is essential for developing effective defense strategies.

Malware

Malware is malicious software designed to damage, disrupt, or gain unauthorized access to computer systems. Types include:

Virus

Self-replicating code that attaches to legitimate programs and spreads when executed.

Worm

Self-replicating malware that spreads independently without user intervention.

Trojan

Disguised as legitimate software but contains hidden malicious functionality.

Ransomware

Encrypts data and demands payment for decryption keys. Increasingly targeted at organizations.

Spyware

Monitors user activity and collects sensitive information without consent.

Adware

Displays unwanted advertisements and can track user behavior for marketing purposes.

Social Engineering Attacks

Social engineering exploits human psychology rather than technical vulnerabilities. These attacks can be highly effective because they target the weakest link: people.

Phishing

Fraudulent emails or messages that impersonate legitimate organizations to steal credentials or information.

Spear Phishing

Targeted phishing attacks directed at specific individuals with personalized information.

Pretexting

Creating a fabricated scenario to gain trust and extract information from the victim.

Baiting

Offering something enticing (like a USB drive) infected with malware to trick users.

Quid Pro Quo

Promising a service or benefit in exchange for information or access.

Web-Based Attacks

SQL Injection

Inserting malicious SQL code into input fields to manipulate databases.

Cross-Site Scripting (XSS)

Injecting malicious scripts into web pages viewed by other users.

Man-in-the-Middle (MitM)

Intercepting communications between two parties to eavesdrop or alter information.

DDoS Attacks

Overwhelming systems with traffic to cause denial of service.

Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs)

APTs are sophisticated, targeted attacks typically conducted by well-funded threat actors. They involve:

  • Reconnaissance and planning over extended periods
  • Exploitation of zero-day vulnerabilities
  • Establishing persistent access to networks
  • Lateral movement within systems
  • Exfiltration of sensitive data
  • Covering tracks to avoid detection

Risk Assessment

Understanding and assessing risk is fundamental to cybersecurity. Risk assessment involves identifying potential threats, evaluating vulnerabilities, and determining the potential impact.

Risk Formula: Risk = Threat Probability × Impact Severity

Organizations prioritize mitigating high-risk scenarios that combine high likelihood with severe potential damage.

Vulnerability Management

A systematic approach to identifying, classifying, remediating, and mitigating vulnerabilities:

  • Identification: Scanning and testing to find vulnerabilities
  • Classification: Determining severity using frameworks like CVSS
  • Prioritization: Focusing on high-risk vulnerabilities first
  • Remediation: Applying patches and fixes
  • Verification: Testing that vulnerabilities are resolved
